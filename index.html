1. Welford’s recurrence

After observing xn, update the count, the running mean, and the sum of squared deviations (often called M2 or SS). Define δn = xn − x̄n−1.

x̄n = x̄n−1 + δn/n
SSn = SSn−1 + δn(xn − x̄n) = SSn−1 + ((n−1)/n)δn2
s2n = SSn/(n−1),   n ≥ 2

The denominator n−1 gives the unbiased sample variance. At n=1, the sample variance is undefined.

Core JavaScript — three update lines

The state is just count, mean, and M2. No array of previous observations is needed.

function add(x) { const delta = x - mean; mean += delta / ++count; M2 += delta * (x - mean); } const sampleVariance = count > 1 ? M2 / (count - 1) : NaN;

The update M2 += delta * (x - mean) is the numerically stable SS recurrence. The same function is used for every hacker’s trajectory below.

How to read the experiment

For each hacker j, calculate x̄j and s²j from that hacker’s n scores using Welford’s recurrence.

Across hackers, compare (1/m)Σx̄j with μ=5 and (1/m)Σs²j with σ²=35/3.

The sample variance uses denominator n−1. This makes each s²j, and therefore their average, unbiased for σ².

What should you conclude?

Center: the average of the hackers’ sample means estimates μ, and the average of their unbiased sample variances estimates σ². With finite m, the observed averages will not match the theoretical values exactly; their discrepancies are Monte Carlo error.

Increasing n: the variability of individual sample means decreases as σ/√n. The variability of the sample variances also decreases (asymptotically at a 1/√n rate). Thus estimates from longer trajectories are more concentrated around the population parameters.

Why this matters: inferential statistics uses sample statistics to learn about unknown population parameters. Understanding their sampling distributions quantifies estimation uncertainty and supports standard errors, confidence intervals, and hypothesis tests.

The exact theoretical SD of the sample variance for this distribution is shown in the simulation table: SD(s²) = √{[μ₄ − ((n−3)/(n−1))σ⁴]/n}. The SD of the average across m hackers is smaller by a factor √m.
